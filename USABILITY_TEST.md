# Usability Test: Ansel MVP

## Intro

Thanks for trying this early prototype. This is a test of the product, not of you. Please think aloud, especially when something feels obvious, confusing, useful, unnecessary, or missing. The session should take 10–20 minutes.

## Who This Is For

- Target participant: anyone who regularly needs a small group to reach agreement
- Context to imagine: your group needs to make a real decision during a voice conversation, with no dedicated facilitator or note-taker
- Prototype run path: start on the landing page and create a new room

## What We Are Testing

Hypothesis: a live, neutral map of agreement and remaining differences helps a group make progress without adding another heavy meeting tool.

Riskiest assumption: participants will see Ansel's evolving synthesis as trustworthy and helpful rather than intrusive or reductive.

Success signal: a new user can create and invite people to a room, understand the two live point streams, and explain how the room helps them reach agreement without facilitator coaching.

## Tasks

1. Start from the landing page. Explain what you think this product does before interacting with it.
2. Create a room for a decision you might genuinely discuss. Add an OKR, reference, or criterion only if it would help.
3. Invite another person using whichever method feels most natural.
4. Join from a second browser or private window and confirm that both members are visible.
5. Start Ansel. Ask “Ansel, can you hear me?” and note how quickly and naturally it answers.
6. While the facilitator is speaking, start talking. Confirm that it stops promptly and listens to you.
7. Discuss the decision for a few minutes. Watch how **Aligned** and **Still different** change independently of the spoken reply.
8. Explain whether the live map represents the conversation fairly and what you would change.
9. Stop when you feel done, stuck, or unsure.

## Observer Notes

For each task, capture:

- Did the participant complete it?
- Where did they hesitate?
- What did they misunderstand?
- What did they trust or distrust?
- What did they say was valuable?
- What did they expect that was missing?

Pay particular attention to whether the simulated landing example explains the product, optional context feels truly optional, inviting is effortless, direct voice replies feel low-latency, interruption works mid-sentence, and the facilitator distinguishes agreement from unresolved differences accurately.

## Rating Questions

1. How clear was the purpose of this prototype? (1–5)
2. How easy was it to create and join a room? (1–5)
3. How accurately did the live map reflect the conversation? (1–5)
4. How useful would this be in a real group decision? (1–5)
5. How likely would you be to use this again if it worked fully? (1–5)

## Open Questions

1. What felt most useful?
2. What felt most confusing or unnecessary?
3. Did Ansel speak too much, too little, or at the right moments?
4. What would make you trust or correct its interpretation?
5. What would need to change before this became genuinely useful?
