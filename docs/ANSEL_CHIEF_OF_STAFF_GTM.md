# Ansel: Chief of Staff GTM

## GTM decision

Launch Ansel through one champion and one recurring ritual:

- **Champion:** Chief of Staff who owns cross-functional decision speed but cannot decide unilaterally.
- **Ritual:** **The Weekly Trade-off Room** — several leaders, competing urgent requests, and insufficient team, budget, or time for all of them.
- **Promise:** Close the trade-off in one meeting, or leave with an explicit reason it cannot yet close.

## Initial ICP

**Primary:** Chiefs of Staff and Heads of Operations at 30–300-person technology and service companies.

Qualifying signals:

- Runs a recurring leadership or resource-allocation meeting with 4–8 functional leaders.
- Is measured on execution speed, alignment, or follow-through.
- Sees decisions reopened because assumptions, dissent, or constraints were not made explicit.
- Has a current trigger: scarce capacity, a delayed launch, missed commitments, hiring constraints, or budget pressure.

**Economic buyer:** Founder, CEO, or COO.  
**Users:** Functional leaders competing for the same capacity.  
**Early-access substitutes:** Fractional COOs and agency delivery leaders with the same decision pattern.

Do not begin with individual PMs: that risks making Ansel look like another backlog tool.

## Urgent job

> When several credible leaders want incompatible things, help me expose the real blocking disagreement and get to an executable, collectively accepted decision without becoming the dictator or the note-taker.

The incumbent is a meeting, a strong facilitator, scattered pre-reads, and follow-up notes. The cost is executive time, slow execution, political friction, and decisions that quietly reopen.

## Positioning

**Category:** Live decision system  
**Positioning line:** Ansel helps leadership teams finish consequential decisions—not merely summarize the meeting.  
**Wedge message:** Five leaders. Three urgent requests. One available team.

Ansel makes the decision state visible in real time: agreed facts, unresolved differences, constraints, assumptions, the current proposal, dissent, and reconsideration triggers. Humans retain authority and explicitly approve the outcome.

Avoid positioning Ansel as an AI meeting recorder, automated decision-maker, consensus generator, or project-management system.

## Initial product ritual: The Weekly Trade-off Room

The Chief of Staff brings one recurring resource-allocation decision. Before the meeting, the group defines the question, objective, criteria, capacity, and decision rule. During the meeting, Ansel maps agreement and disagreement, tests the proposal against capacity, and surfaces the assumption blocking closure. The room ends with an approved allocation—or an explicit non-decision with missing evidence and an owner.

The durable artifact is a decision receipt containing the decision, accepted trade-offs, recorded dissent, assumptions, and reconsideration triggers.

## Founder-led acquisition motion

1. **Prospect on pain, not interest.** Ask: “What decision has your leadership team discussed more than once without closing?”
2. **Run a 20-minute decision-latency audit.** Confirm the decision is recurring, cross-functional, capacity-constrained, and costly to delay.
3. **Sell a four-week Decision Velocity Pilot.** Personally configure and facilitate 3–4 real Weekly Trade-off Rooms; do not lead with a generic software demo.
4. **Review evidence with the sponsor.** Compare baseline and pilot decision latency, reopen rate, corrections, participant confidence, and repeat demand.
5. **Convert the ritual.** Move the team to a subscription only when the meeting genuinely recurs. Expand later into launch go/no-go, hiring debriefs, and budget allocation.

## Channels

Prioritize channels where the champion already discusses executive alignment:

1. Warm introductions through founders, operators, investors, and portfolio-support teams.
2. Highly targeted LinkedIn outreach to Chiefs of Staff with a visible growth, reorganization, launch, or planning trigger.
3. Chief of Staff communities and small practitioner roundtables using an anonymized “stuck decision clinic.”
4. Fractional COOs, executive coaches, and operating partners as design-partner and referral channels.
5. Founder content: short decision-latency audits and before/after teardowns of repeatedly reopened decisions.

The acquisition asset is the outcome, not an AI demo. Every shared decision receipt should offer a clear path to create another Ansel room.

## Pricing hypothesis

- **Four-week concierge pilot:** $250–500 for 3–4 facilitated rooms.
- **One recurring decision team:** $99/month.
- **Multiple teams:** $249/month, including templates and decision history.
- Unlimited participants; never charge per attendee.

Charge for pilots early. Free trials measure curiosity; paid pilots test whether decision latency is painful enough to fund.

## First 10 pilots: scorecard

| Signal | Continue threshold |
|---|---:|
| Rooms ending in an approved decision or explicit non-decision | ≥70% |
| Median time-to-decision improvement | ≥30% |
| Decisions reopened within 7 days | Lower than baseline |
| Companies requesting another room | ≥5 of 10 |
| Companies willing to pay | ≥5 of 10 |
| Material corrections to Ansel's interpretation | <20% of rooms |
| Reports that Ansel manufactured agreement | 0 |

Also track acquisition efficiency: at least 20% of qualified conversations should become paid pilots, and at least 50% of completed pilots should convert or pre-commit to another paid cycle.

## Validation and kill criteria

**Validate the wedge** after 10 real pilots if the continue thresholds are met and at least three teams adopt the Weekly Trade-off Room as a recurring ritual without founder prompting.

**Iterate the workflow or messaging** if teams value the session but corrections are frequent, participants hesitate to speak candidly, or the Chief of Staff cannot deploy Ansel without executive permission.

**Kill the Chief of Staff wedge** if fewer than five pilots pay, fewer than five teams repeat, or decision latency/reopen rates do not improve. If buyers only value the founder's facilitation, treat that as a services signal—not product validation. If use remains episodic across teams, abandon the subscription assumption and test pay-per-decision pricing before expanding the market.
